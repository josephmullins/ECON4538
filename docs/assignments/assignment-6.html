Assignment 6

This assignment works with the technology of skill formation from Lecture 6 (Cunha and Heckman 2007; Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach 2010). A child is born with initial skill \(\theta_1\). A parent invests \(I_1\) in early childhood and \(I_2\) in late childhood. Skills evolve according to

\[ \theta_{2} = \left[\gamma_1\theta_1^{\phi_1} + (1-\gamma_1)I_1^{\phi_1}\right]^{1/\phi_1}, \qquad \theta_{3} = \left[\gamma_2\theta_2^{\phi_2} + (1-\gamma_2)I_2^{\phi_2}\right]^{1/\phi_2} \]

where \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in(0,1)\) and \(\phi_1,\phi_2 < 1\). When \(\phi_t \to 0\) the corresponding stage is Cobb-Douglas: \(\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t^{\gamma_t}I_t^{1-\gamma_t}\). The parent has a fixed budget \(B\) (in period-1 units) and can borrow and save at gross interest rate \(R\), so that

\[ I_1 + \frac{I_2}{R} = B. \]

The parent chooses \((I_1, I_2)\) to maximize the child’s adult skill \(\theta_3\). Unless stated otherwise, use \(\gamma_1 = 0.3\), \(\gamma_2 = 0.7\) and \(R = 1.1\).

  1. Optimal timing (analytical). Suppose \(\phi_1 = \phi_2 = \phi\).
    1. Substitute \(\theta_2\) into the second-stage technology to write \(\theta_3\) as a function of \(\theta_1\), \(I_1\) and \(I_2\).
    2. Show that the optimal ratio of early to late investment is \[ \frac{I_1}{I_2} = \left(\frac{\gamma_2(1-\gamma_1)}{(1-\gamma_2)R}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\phi}}. \]
    3. Explain in words what \(\gamma_2(1-\gamma_1)\) and \((1-\gamma_2)R\) represent. How does the ratio change as \(\phi \to -\infty\)? As \(\phi \to 1\)?
    4. Does the optimal ratio depend on the child’s initial skill \(\theta_1\)? Does the child’s final skill \(\theta_3\)?
  2. Optimal timing (numerical). Write a function that, given \(\theta_1\), \(B\) and the parameters, finds the share of the budget \(s = I_1/B\) that maximizes \(\theta_3\).
    1. Check your code against the formula from question 1 for \(\phi \in \{-1, 0, 0.5\}\).
    2. With \(B = 1\), plot the optimal early share \(s\) and the resulting \(\theta_3\) against \(\phi \in [-2, 0.8]\), for \(\theta_1 \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1, 2\}\). Describe what you find.
    3. Cunha, Heckman, and Schennach (2010) find that, for cognitive skills, the elasticity of substitution is higher early in childhood than later. Set \(\phi_1 = 0.5\) and \(\phi_2 = -0.5\) and plot the optimal early share against \(\theta_1 \in [0.1, 2]\). Does the answer to 1(d) still hold? Give some intuition.
  3. The cost of delaying remediation. Consider an advantaged child with \(\theta_1 = 1\) who receives \(I_1 = I_2 = 1\), and let \(\bar{\theta}\) be the adult skill this child reaches. A disadvantaged child starts with \(\theta_1 = 0.5\). Assume \(\phi_1 = \phi_2 = \phi\).
    1. Early remediation: find the smallest budget \(B\) such that, investing optimally, the disadvantaged child reaches \(\bar{\theta}\).
    2. Delayed remediation: suppose the disadvantaged child only receives \(I_1 = 0.5\) in early childhood, and help arrives only in late childhood. Find the \(I_2\) needed to reach \(\bar{\theta}\), and the total cost \(0.5 + I_2/R\).
    3. Report both costs and their ratio for \(\phi \in \{-0.5, -0.25, 0, 0.25, 0.5\}\). What happens as \(\phi\) falls?
    4. Show that when \(\phi < 0\), there is an upper bound on \(\theta_3\) that no amount of late investment can exceed. What is it? Connect your answer to the idea of a critical period.
  4. Measurement error (optional, extra credit). In the data we never observe skills directly, only test scores (Lecture 6). Set \(\phi_1 = 0\), so that \(\log\theta_2 = \gamma_1\log\theta_1 + (1-\gamma_1)\log I_1\).
    1. Simulate \(N = 5{,}000\) children with \(\log\theta_1 \sim N(0, 0.5^2)\) and \(\log I_1 = 0.3\log\theta_1 + \nu\), \(\nu\sim N(0,0.5^2)\) (better-endowed children receive more investment). Compute \(\theta_2\).
    2. Generate two test scores \(Z_j = \log\theta_1 + \varepsilon_j\), \(j=1,2\), and an outcome measure \(Y = \log\theta_2 + u\), with independent normal errors (\(\text{sd}(\varepsilon_j) = 0.5\), \(\text{sd}(u) = 0.3\)).
    3. Regress \(Y\) on a constant, \(Z_1\) and \(\log I_1\). Compare the estimates of \(\gamma_1\) and \(1-\gamma_1\) to the truth. Why is the coefficient on investment also biased?
    4. Re-estimate by two-stage least squares, using \(Z_2\) as an instrument for \(Z_1\). Explain why this works.
